Durations work as in Obsidian (bases-page#15). Subtracting two dates gives a duration, shown like Obsidian shows it (`9 days`, `an hour`, `a month`, without a sign), instead of a number of milliseconds. Its fields `.days`, `.hours`, `.minutes`, `.seconds`, `.milliseconds`, `.weeks` are fractional and keep the sign (`(b - a).days.round()`); `.months` and `.years` count calendar months from today. `duration("1 week")`, `date + "1d"`, `date - duration("1M")` and `date + (a - b)` work; `duration * 2` and `duration / 2` stay durations; durations compare with durations, with duration strings (`> "1w"`) and with milliseconds. **Changed, as in Obsidian:** `duration()` accepts one whole number and one unit (`1d`, `2 weeks`, `90 seconds`; not `1y 2M` or `1.5h`); `2 * duration`, `date + 1`, `number(duration)` and `duration.round()` are errors, and a formula's error is shown in its cell instead of an empty value; date-only strings in `date()` are local midnight like datetimes (they were UTC midnight, which put `date("2024-03-10 12:30") - date("2024-03-01")` an hour off). Date properties such as `due: 2024-03-05` are dates in formulas, so `today() - due` works.
